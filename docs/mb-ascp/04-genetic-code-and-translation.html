MB 4. Genetic code and translation

MB-ASCP
Codons and wobble, tRNA charging, bacterial and eukaryotic ribosomes, translation initiation, elongation, termination, fidelity, and variant interpretation.
Author

Vahhab Piranfar

Published

November 11, 2026

MB(ASCP) study notes · Lesson 4

Translation converts the nucleotide sequence of an mRNA into the amino-acid sequence of a polypeptide. For the exam, keep four directions straight: the ribosome reads mRNA 5′→3′, codon and anticodon pair antiparallel, the protein grows N-terminus→C-terminus, and new aminoacyl-tRNAs normally enter the A site. The initiator tRNA is the exception: it starts in the P site.

The genetic code

A codon is a group of three nucleotides in mRNA. Four bases can make (4^3 = 64) codons: 61 sense codons specify the 20 standard amino acids, and three are stop signals.

Property What it means Exam consequence
Triplet Three bases form one codon Read the mRNA in groups of three
Degenerate Several codons can specify one amino acid UUU and UUC both mean phenylalanine
Nearly universal Most organisms use the same assignments Mitochondrial and specialized exceptions exist
Non-overlapping A base belongs to one codon in a reading frame A frameshift changes every downstream triplet
Commaless Codons are read continuously The start point determines the frame

AUG is the usual start codon. It specifies methionine; bacterial initiation uses N-formylmethionine (fMet). The stop codons are UAA, UAG, and UGA. A stop codon does not add an amino acid. A release factor recognizes it.

Example:

5′-AUG UUU GGC UAA-3′ → Met–Phe–Gly–STOP

The product contains three amino acids. UGA can be recoded as selenocysteine in the correct sequence context with specialized machinery, so “universal” and “always stop” are useful rules, not exception-free laws.

Original schematic. A four-codon message produces a three-amino-acid peptide because stop is a signal, not a residue.

Codon, anticodon, and wobble

tRNA is the adaptor between nucleic acid and protein. Its anticodon recognizes the mRNA codon. Its conserved 3′-CCA end carries the amino acid on the terminal adenosine.

Codon and anticodon pair antiparallel:

  • mRNA codon: 5′-AUG-3′
  • Paired anticodon: 3′-UAC-5′
  • The same anticodon written conventionally 5′→3′: 5′-CAU-3′

Always inspect the printed direction before choosing an anticodon. Complementing AUG to UAC without checking direction is the common trap.

The first two codon positions usually demand standard Watson–Crick geometry. The third codon base is more flexible. This is wobble, corresponding to the first base at the 5′ end of the anticodon. Wobble lets one tRNA recognize several synonymous codons and reduces the number of distinct tRNAs a cell needs.

5′ base of anticodon Possible 3′ base(s) of codon
C G
A U
U A or G
G C or U
Inosine (I) U, C, or A

An anticodon 5′-IGC-3′ can recognize 5′-GCU-3′, 5′-GCC-3′, and 5′-GCA-3′. The code has not changed; the pairing mechanism is flexible.

Degeneracy and wobble are not synonyms. Degeneracy is the fact that several codons encode the same amino acid. Wobble is how one anticodon can physically recognize several of those codons.

Charging the tRNA

Before a tRNA enters translation, an aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase (aaRS) attaches the correct amino acid. Charging is a separate accuracy checkpoint from ribosomal decoding.

  1. Amino acid + ATP → aminoacyl-AMP + PPi
  2. Aminoacyl-AMP + tRNA → aminoacyl-tRNA + AMP

ATP is converted to AMP, and PPi is hydrolyzed. That costs the equivalent of two high-energy phosphate bonds. The amino acid’s carboxyl group forms an ester bond to a hydroxyl on the terminal A of the 3′-CCA end. Its amino group remains free for peptide-bond formation.

Many synthetases have an editing site that removes an incorrectly activated amino acid or hydrolyzes a mischarged tRNA. This matters because the ribosome mainly checks codon–anticodon pairing. If the correct anticodon carries the wrong amino acid, the ribosome may accept it and put the wrong residue into the protein.

Checkpoint What it verifies Error if it fails
Aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase Correct amino acid on its cognate tRNA Correct anticodon delivers the wrong amino acid
Ribosomal decoding center Correct codon–anticodon geometry Near-cognate tRNA enters the chain

Charging prepares the adaptor. Decoding later checks the anticodon. Those are different fidelity checkpoints.

Ribosomes and the A, P, and E sites

The small subunit decodes mRNA. The large subunit contains the peptidyl-transferase center.

Bacterial Eukaryotic cytoplasmic
Complete ribosome 70S 80S
Small subunit 30S: 16S rRNA 40S: 18S rRNA
Large subunit 50S: 23S + 5S rRNA 60S: 28S + 5.8S + 5S rRNA

S means Svedberg sedimentation coefficient. It depends on size, shape, and density and is not additive: 30S + 50S makes 70S, not 80S.

The three sites are easiest as one sentence: A accepts, P carries the peptide, E exits.

  • A site: incoming aminoacyl-tRNA
  • P site: tRNA carrying the growing polypeptide
  • E site: deacylated tRNA before departure

In bacteria, 16S rRNA belongs to the 30S decoding subunit. 23S rRNA belongs to the 50S subunit and performs the central peptidyl-transferase chemistry. The ribosome is therefore a ribozyme.

Initiation fixes the reading frame

Initiation puts the start codon in the correct position and places the special initiator tRNA directly in the P site.

Bacteria

  1. The 30S subunit binds initiation factors.
  2. The mRNA Shine–Dalgarno sequence base-pairs with 16S rRNA and positions AUG.
  3. IF-2–GTP delivers fMet-tRNA to the P site.
  4. IF-1 organizes the complex; IF-3 prevents premature 50S joining.
  5. The 50S subunit joins, GTP is hydrolyzed, and a 70S initiation complex remains.

Eukaryotic cytoplasm

The 40S complex is recruited to the 5′ cap through initiation factors including eIF4E in eIF4F. eIF2–GTP helps load non-formylated initiator Met-tRNA. The complex scans 5′→3′ for an AUG in a favorable Kozak context, then the 60S subunit joins to form an 80S ribosome.

Feature Bacteria Eukaryotes
Start positioning Shine–Dalgarno pairs with 16S rRNA 5′ cap recruitment, scanning, Kozak sequence
Initiator amino acid fMet Met, not formylated
Initiator delivery IF-2–GTP eIF2–GTP
Completed ribosome 70S 80S

“All tRNAs enter the A site” is false. Elongator tRNAs do. The initiator tRNA starts in P.

Elongation: delivery, bond, movement

The cycle must stay in this order:

  1. Delivery and decoding. Bacterial EF-Tu–GTP carries an aminoacyl-tRNA to A. Correct pairing triggers GTP hydrolysis and accommodation. EF-Ts regenerates EF-Tu–GTP.
  2. Peptide-bond formation. The amino group on the A-site amino acid attacks the ester-linked peptide on the P-site tRNA. The peptide transfers to the A-site tRNA. Bacterial 23S rRNA catalyzes the reaction.
  3. Translocation. EF-G–GTP moves the ribosome one codon. Peptidyl-tRNA moves A→P; empty tRNA moves P→E and exits. A is free again.
Bacterial factor Role Eukaryotic counterpart
EF-Tu Delivers aminoacyl-tRNA to A eEF1A
EF-Ts Exchanges GDP for GTP on EF-Tu eEF1B complex
EF-G Translocation eEF2

Each elongation cycle uses two GTP in this simplified accounting: one for EF-Tu selection and one for EF-G translocation. This is in addition to the ATP→AMP cost of charging.

After bond formation the peptide is briefly on the A-site tRNA. EF-G then moves that tRNA into P.

Termination and recycling

Termination begins when UAA, UAG, or UGA reaches A. There is no ordinary charged tRNA for stop. A release factor enters, and water hydrolyzes the ester bond between the completed chain and the P-site tRNA.

Factor Recognition
Bacterial RF1 UAA and UAG
Bacterial RF2 UAA and UGA
Bacterial RF3 GTPase assisting release-factor turnover
Eukaryotic eRF1 All three stop codons
Eukaryotic eRF3 GTPase assisting termination

After release, bacterial RRF, EF-G, and IF-3 recycle the complex. Eukaryotes use ABCE1 and additional factors.

A premature termination codon can trigger nonsense-mediated mRNA decay (NMD). NMD lowers the amount of an mRNA that could produce a truncated protein. It is context-dependent and is an mRNA surveillance pathway, not ordinary termination.

Translation errors, variants, and laboratory evidence

A translation error affects a particular protein molecule without necessarily changing DNA or mRNA. A genetic variant changes the encoded sequence and can recur in every transcript or protein produced from that allele.

Event Sequence effect Likely protein consequence
Missense variant One sense codon becomes another One amino-acid substitution
Nonsense variant Sense codon becomes stop Truncation and possibly NMD
Frameshift Insertion/deletion not divisible by three New downstream frame, often early stop
In-frame indel Insertion/deletion divisible by three Residues added or removed; frame preserved
Translation error DNA may be unchanged Wrong residue in an individual protein molecule

One method rarely answers every clinical question:

Method Best question Limitation
Sanger sequencing Is a targeted DNA variant present? Does not prove protein dysfunction
NGS Which variants are present across many regions? Coverage and interpretation limits remain
RT-PCR/RNA analysis Is transcript structure or splicing altered? Requires suitable RNA and assay design
qPCR Is nucleic-acid abundance changed? Abundance does not prove protein function
Western blot Is protein abundance or apparent size altered? Does not establish activity
Enzyme assay Is function impaired? May not identify the causal DNA change

For a premature stop, separate three questions: confirm the DNA variant; test whether the RNA is spliced differently or degraded by NMD; then determine whether protein is absent, shortened, unstable, or inactive.

Say these without looking

  • The ribosome reads mRNA 5′→3′; protein grows N→C.
  • AUG starts. UAA, UAG, and UGA stop. Bacterial initiator amino acid is fMet.
  • Wobble is the third codon base and first 5′ anticodon base. Degeneracy is not wobble.
  • Aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase charges 3′-CCA. ATP→AMP costs two high-energy bond equivalents.
  • 70S = 30S + 50S; 80S = 40S + 60S. S values are not additive.
  • 16S decodes; 23S catalyzes peptide-bond formation.
  • A accepts, P holds peptide, E exits. The initiator tRNA starts in P.
  • Shine–Dalgarno is bacterial. Cap scanning and Kozak context are eukaryotic.
  • EF-Tu delivers, EF-Ts reloads EF-Tu, EF-G translocates.
  • Peptide bond first, translocation second.
  • RF1 recognizes UAA/UAG; RF2 recognizes UAA/UGA; eRF1 recognizes all three.
  • Missense substitutes, nonsense stops, frameshift changes the downstream frame.

Check yourself

Ten original questions. Answer all of them before checking; the explanation appears with the key.

1. Which statement correctly describes translation directionality?

The ribosome advances along mRNA 5′→3′. Each new residue is added to the C end, so the polypeptide grows from N terminus to C terminus.

2. The mRNA codon is 5′-AUG-3′. Which anticodon is correctly written 5′→3′?

The paired anticodon is 3′-UAC-5′. Reverse it when writing 5′→3′: 5′-CAU-3′.

3. Wobble occurs between which positions?

Codon and anticodon are antiparallel. Flexibility at codon position 3 corresponds to the 5′-most anticodon base.

4. Why can a mischarged tRNA cause an incorrect amino acid to be incorporated despite correct codon pairing?

Aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase establishes amino-acid identity. The ribosome can accept a correctly pairing anticodon even when the attached amino acid is wrong.

5. Which bacterial rRNA contains the principal peptidyl-transferase catalytic activity?

23S rRNA is in the 50S large subunit and catalyzes peptide transfer. The 16S rRNA belongs to the 30S decoding subunit.

6. During normal initiation, the initiator tRNA first occupies which site?

The special initiator tRNA starts in P. Later aminoacyl-tRNAs enter through A.

7. Which bacterial factor delivers an aminoacyl-tRNA to the A site?

EF-Tu–GTP delivers and helps select the charged tRNA. EF-G translocates after peptide-bond formation.

8. Immediately after peptide-bond formation but before translocation, the growing peptide is attached to the tRNA in which site?

Peptide transfer moves the chain from P-site tRNA to A-site tRNA. EF-G then translocates that peptidyl-tRNA A→P.

9. Which bacterial release factor recognizes UAA and UGA?

RF2 recognizes UAA and UGA. RF1 recognizes UAA and UAG. Eukaryotic eRF1 recognizes all three stops.

10. A one-nucleotide deletion in a coding region most directly causes which variant class?

An indel not divisible by three shifts the downstream reading frame. A three-base deletion would be in-frame.
