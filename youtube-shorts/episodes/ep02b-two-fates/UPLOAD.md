**Title**
Two Fates: One Enzyme Decides Whether an Antibiotic Works

**Description**
The same antibiotic enters two bacteria through the same door. One cell carries a beta-lactamase, the other does not. Watched side by side, frame for frame, they share the first fifteen seconds and then part: in the first cell the enzyme opens the drug's beta-lactam ring and releases it, harmless; in the second the drug opens its ring on the cell-wall enzyme PBP1b and stays bound, the wall stops growing, opens, and the inner membrane pushes through.

Everything shown is a real structure. OmpF porin and E. coli LPS come from a molecular dynamics simulation (CHARMM-GUI, GROMACS). TEM-1 beta-lactamase from a 10 ns MD run; the bound and hydrolyzed cephalosporin poses from PDB 5UJ3 placed on the active site. PBP1b from PDB 3VMA; the acyl-enzyme pose from PDB 2C5W placed on the conserved SxxK / SxN / KTG motifs. AcrB from MemProtMD (4DX5). The peptidoglycan mesh and the lysis are stylized. Rendered in Blender with Molecular Nodes.

Narration: synthetic voice (Azure neural). Music: "Rain Over Kyoto Station" by The Mini Vandals (YouTube Audio Library).
